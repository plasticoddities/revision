Popular Culture

Revise popular culture through the teen film focus studied in class.

1. Nature of popular culture

Popular culture is shared and widely consumed by groups at a particular time. Four characteristics: commercial products/paraphernalia, local-to-global development, widespread consumer access, constant change/evolution.

2. Creation and development

Popular cultures emerge in particular social contexts and develop over time.

Evidence: Rebel Without a Cause for 1950s youth rebellion; The Breakfast Club for teen identity groups; High School Musical for commercialisation; Clueless for commodified youth identity.
3. Commercialisation vs commodification

Commercialisation: producing/promoting cultural products for profit. Commodification: turning cultural ideas, symbols or identities into things that can be bought and sold.

4. Local to global

Revise globalisation, cultural diffusion, adaptation, Westernisation and technology.

Class examples: Squid Game and Netflix; altered films for Chinese audiences; McDonald's as Westernisation plus localisation.
5. Consumption and interpretation

Audiences are active meaning-makers. Interpretations vary with values, culture, experience and context.

Main evidence: 10 Things I Hate About You (1999) as main source; Do Revenge (2022) as comparison for identity, power, relationships, conflict and gender.
6. Power, control and impact

Media organisations, corporations and audiences shape popular culture. Control can occur through ownership, production, distribution, algorithms and curation.

Class examples: TikTok's For You Page and Disney.
7. Future directions

Use trend → impact → future → judgement. Consider streaming, algorithms, social media, changing values and commercialisation, while also identifying continuity.
